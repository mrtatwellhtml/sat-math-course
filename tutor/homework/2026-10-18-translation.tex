\PartHead{Part A --- Retention}{No calculator. 15 minutes.}
\sitting{Sunday}
Three weeks of work sits behind these ten. Keep the brackets around every substitution.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=20pt,itemsep=5pt]
\item $-9-(-4)=$ \ifANSWERS\else\underline{\hspace{3.5cm}}\fi \ans{$-5$}
\item Expand: $-3(x-5)$ \ifANSWERS\ans{$=-3x+15$}\else\ \underline{\hspace{5.4cm}}\fi
\item Expand and simplify: $-(4x-7)+x$ \ifANSWERS\ans{$=-3x+7$}\else\ \underline{\hspace{4cm}}\fi
\item A line passes through $(-2,7)$ and $(2,-1)$. What is its slope?
  \ifANSWERS\ans{$-2$. \ $\dfrac{-1-7}{2-(-2)}=\dfrac{-8}{4}=-2$.}\else\ \underline{\hspace{4.4cm}}\fi
\item A line is given by $3x+4y=8$. What is its slope?
  \ifANSWERS\ans{$-3/4$. \ $y=-\dfrac{3}{4}x+2$.}\else\ \underline{\hspace{4.4cm}}\fi
\item A line is given by $5x-2y=10$. What is the $y$-coordinate of its $y$-intercept?
  \ifANSWERS\ans{$-5$. \ Put $x=0$: $-2y=10$.}\else\ \underline{\hspace{3.4cm}}\fi
\item Solve by substitution: $y=2x-3$ and $3x+y=12$. Give $x$ and $y$. \spr
  \ans{\\ $x=3$, $y=3$. \ $3x+(2x-3)=12$, so $5x=15$. Check: $3(3)+3=12$.}

\item Solve by elimination: $4x+y=14$ and $2x-y=4$. Give $x$ and $y$. \spr
  \ans{\\ $x=3$, $y=2$. \ Adding the two equations kills $y$: $6x=18$. Then $y=14-12=2$.}

\item Solve $2x+7<1$. \spr
  \ans{\\ $x<-3$. \ $2x<-6$. No flip here --- you divided by a positive. Check: $x=-4$ gives $-1<1$, true.}

\item Solve $5-3x\ge20$. \spr
  \ans{\\ $x\le-5$. \ $-3x\ge15$, then divide by $-3$ \emph{and flip}. Check: $x=-6$ gives $23\ge20$, true.}
\end{enumerate}

\PartHead{Part B --- Setups only}{Calculator allowed. 25 minutes.}
\sitting{Tuesday}
\textbf{Do not solve any of these.} For each one you write two things and then stop:
first name the unknown on the \emph{Let} line, then write the equation (or the pair of
equations) on the line under it. No arithmetic. No answer.

You can do the arithmetic already. What costs you marks is building the equation, so
this sitting practises only that. An equation you reached without naming the unknown
does not count this week.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=20pt,itemsep=7pt]
\item A moving company charges a flat \$75 for the van plus \$0.60 for every mile
  driven. A move cost \$153 in total. Write an equation for the number of miles.
  \setuplines
  \ans{\\ Let $m$ be the number of miles. \ $75+0.60m=153$. \ (True $m=130$:
  $75+0.60(130)=153$.)}

\item A printing shop charges an \$18 setup fee plus \$0.35 for each flyer. An order
  cost \$81. Write an equation for the number of flyers.
  \setuplines
  \ans{\\ Let $f$ be the number of flyers. \ $18+0.35f=81$. \ (True $f=180$:
  $18+0.35(180)=81$.)}

\item A theatre sold 40 tickets and took \$395. Adult tickets cost \$12 and child
  tickets cost \$7. Write a \emph{pair} of equations.
  \setuplines
  \ans{\\ Let $a$ be the number of adult tickets and $c$ the number of child tickets.
  \ $a+c=40$ and $12a+7c=395$. \ (True $a=23$, $c=17$: $23+17=40$ and
  $276+119=395$.)}

\item A box holds 30 pens. Black pens cost \$1.20 each and blue pens cost \$0.80 each,
  and the whole box is worth \$30. Write a \emph{pair} of equations.
  \setuplines
  \ans{\\ Let $b$ be the number of black pens and $u$ the number of blue pens.
  \ $b+u=30$ and $1.20b+0.80u=30$. \ (True $b=15$, $u=15$: $18+12=30$.)}

\item A train travels at a steady 56 miles per hour and covers 280 miles. Write an
  equation for the time taken.
  \setuplines
  \ans{\\ Let $h$ be the number of hours. \ $56h=280$. \ (True $h=5$: $56(5)=280$.)}

\item A tank holds 540 litres and a pump removes 18 litres each minute. At some moment
  90 litres remain. Write an equation for the time the pump has run.
  \setuplines
  \ans{\\ Let $m$ be the number of minutes. \ $540-18m=90$. \ (True $m=25$:
  $540-450=90$.) The pump \emph{removes}, so the term is subtracted.}

\item In one class, 35\% of the students chose Spanish, and 14 students chose Spanish.
  Write an equation for the number of students in the class.
  \setuplines
  \ans{\\ Let $n$ be the number of students. \ $0.35n=14$. \ (True $n=40$:
  $0.35(40)=14$.)}

\item \textbf{Reminder: you are still not solving these.} A jacket is reduced by 20\%
  and sells for \$68. Write an equation for the original price.
  \setuplines
  \ans{\\ Let $p$ be the original price in dollars. \ $0.80p=68$. \ (True $p=85$:
  $0.80(85)=68$.) A 20\% reduction leaves 80\%, so the multiplier is $0.80$, not $0.20$.}

\item A club raised its membership fee by 12\%, and the new fee is \$504. Write an
  equation for the old fee.
  \setuplines
  \ans{\\ Let $f$ be the old fee in dollars. \ $1.12f=504$. \ (True $f=450$:
  $1.12(450)=504$.) An increase of 12\% multiplies by $1.12$.}

\item Three consecutive integers add to 84. Write an equation.
  \setuplines
  \ans{\\ Let $n$ be the smallest integer. \ $n+(n+1)+(n+2)=84$. \ (True $n=27$:
  $27+28+29=84$.)}

\item Two consecutive odd integers add to 92. Write an equation.
  \setuplines
  \ans{\\ Let $n$ be the smaller odd integer. \ $n+(n+2)=92$. \ (True $n=45$:
  $45+47=92$.) Consecutive \emph{odd} integers differ by 2, not 1.}

\item A rectangle has a length that is 5 cm more than twice its width, and its
  perimeter is 70 cm. Write an equation in the width alone.
  \setuplines
  \ans{\\ Let $w$ be the width in cm, so the length is $2w+5$.
  \ $2(2w+5)+2w=70$. \ (True $w=10$, length 25: $2(25)+2(10)=70$.)}
\end{enumerate}

% ------------------------------------------------ PART C
\PartHead{Part C --- The two traps}{Calculator allowed. 25 minutes.}
\sitting{Thursday}
Two mistakes account for most of what goes wrong when you translate.

\textbf{The reversal trap.} ``7 less than $x$'' is $x-7$. Never $7-x$. The thing you
start with is written first, and the amount taken away comes after the minus sign.

\textbf{The direction trap.} ``Twice as many apples as pears'', with $a$ apples and
$p$ pears, is $a=2p$. The multiplier goes on the \emph{smaller} side, because that is
the side that needs building up to match.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=20pt,itemsep=9pt]
\item Which expression represents ``seven less than a number $x$''?\\
  \makebox[3.4cm][l]{A) $7-x$}\makebox[3.4cm][l]{B) $x-7$}%
  \makebox[3.4cm][l]{C) $7x$}\makebox[3cm][l]{D) $\dfrac{7}{x}$}
  \ans{\\ B. A is the reversal: it reads the words in the order they appear. C reads
  ``less'' as a product. D reads it as a quotient.}

\item A number $n$ decreased by 12 is equal to 5 times the number. Which equation says
  that?\\
  \makebox[3.6cm][l]{A) $12-n=5n$}\makebox[3.6cm][l]{B) $n-12=5n$}%
  \makebox[3.6cm][l]{C) $n-12=n+5$}\makebox[3.4cm][l]{D) $12-n=\dfrac{n}{5}$}
  \ans{\\ B. ``Decreased by'' takes 12 off $n$, so $n$ comes first. A reverses the
  subtraction and gives $n=2$ instead of $n=-3$. C reads ``5 times'' as ``plus 5''.
  D does both wrongs at once.}

\item Which expression represents ``9 subtracted from 4 times $k$''?\\
  \makebox[3.6cm][l]{A) $9-4k$}\makebox[3.6cm][l]{B) $4k-9$}%
  \makebox[3.6cm][l]{C) $4(k-9)$}\makebox[3.4cm][l]{D) $4-9k$}
  \ans{\\ B. ``Subtracted from'' means the 9 comes off the $4k$. A is the reversal.
  C subtracts before multiplying. D swaps the two numbers.}

\item A number $a$ is 6 less than three times a number $b$. Which equation says that?\\
  \makebox[3.6cm][l]{A) $a=6-3b$}\makebox[3.6cm][l]{B) $a=3b-6$}%
  \makebox[3.6cm][l]{C) $a=3(b-6)$}\makebox[3.4cm][l]{D) $6-a=3b$}
  \ans{\\ B. Build $3b$ first, then take 6 off it. A is the reversal. C takes the 6
  off before multiplying. D takes the 6 off the wrong quantity.}

\item A student is told ``the cost $c$ is \$8 less than the price $p$'' and writes
  $c=8-p$. In one sentence, say why that is wrong, then write the correct equation.\\
  \underline{\hspace{\linewidth}}\\*[7pt]
  \underline{\hspace{\linewidth}}
  \ans{\\ It is the reversal trap: the 8 is what gets taken off the price, so the price
  must come first. Correct: $c=p-8$. Test it --- a price of \$20 gives a cost of \$12,
  while $8-20$ gives $-\$12$, and a cost cannot be negative.}

\item A shelter has three times as many cats $c$ as dogs $d$. Which equation says that?\\
  \makebox[3.4cm][l]{A) $c=3d$}\makebox[3.4cm][l]{B) $3c=d$}%
  \makebox[3.4cm][l]{C) $c+3=d$}\makebox[3cm][l]{D) $c=d+3$}
  \ans{\\ A. Test with 4 dogs: there are 12 cats, and $12=3(4)$ holds. B is the
  direction trap --- it would put 12 dogs with 4 cats. C and D read ``times'' as
  ``more than''.}

\item A theatre has 5 times as many standard seats $s$ as premium seats $p$. Which
  equation says that?\\
  \makebox[3.4cm][l]{A) $5s=p$}\makebox[3.4cm][l]{B) $s=5p$}%
  \makebox[3.4cm][l]{C) $s=p+5$}\makebox[3cm][l]{D) $p=s+5$}
  \ans{\\ B. Test with 30 premium seats: 150 standard, and $150=5(30)$ holds. A is the
  direction trap. C and D turn a multiplier into an addition.}

\item A recipe uses twice as much flour $f$ as sugar $g$, both measured in cups. Which
  equation says that?\\
  \makebox[3.4cm][l]{A) $f=2g$}\makebox[3.4cm][l]{B) $2f=g$}%
  \makebox[3.4cm][l]{C) $f=g+2$}\makebox[3cm][l]{D) $fg=2$}
  \ans{\\ A. Test with 3 cups of sugar: 6 cups of flour, and $6=2(3)$ holds. B is the
  direction trap. C reads ``twice'' as ``2 more''. D multiplies the two amounts
  together.}

\item A class has 4 times as many juniors $j$ as seniors $n$. A student writes $4j=n$.
  In one sentence, say why that is wrong, then write the correct equation.\\
  \underline{\hspace{\linewidth}}\\*[7pt]
  \underline{\hspace{\linewidth}}
  \ans{\\ It points the multiplier the wrong way: $4j=n$ says the seniors are the larger
  group. Correct: $j=4n$. Test it --- 5 seniors give 20 juniors, and $20=4(5)$ holds,
  while $4(20)=80$ is nowhere near 5.}

\item A jar has half as many red marbles $r$ as blue marbles $b$. A student writes
  $r=2b$. In one sentence, say why that is wrong, then write the correct equation.\\
  \underline{\hspace{\linewidth}}\\*[7pt]
  \underline{\hspace{\linewidth}}
  \ans{\\ It doubles the group that is meant to be the smaller one. Correct:
  $r=\dfrac{b}{2}$, which can also be written $b=2r$. Test it --- 10 blue marbles give
  5 red, while $r=2b$ would give 20.}
\end{enumerate}

% ------------------------------------------------ PART D
\PartHead{Part D --- Set up and solve}{Calculator allowed. 15 minutes.}
\sitting{Saturday}
Now you do both halves. Name the unknown, write the equation, solve it, and then read
your answer back against the story to check it makes sense.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=20pt,itemsep=10pt]
\item A gym charges a one-time joining fee of \$40 plus \$29 for each month of
  membership. A member has paid \$359 in total. For how many months has he been a
  member? \spr
  \ans{\\ $11$. \ Let $m$ be the months: $40+29m=359$, so $29m=319$ and $m=11$.
  Check: $40+29(11)=359$.}

\item A theatre sold 60 tickets and took \$732. Adult tickets cost \$15 and student
  tickets cost \$9. How many student tickets were sold? \spr
  \ans{\\ $28$. \ Let $a$ and $s$ be the counts: $a+s=60$ and $15a+9s=732$. Substituting
  $a=60-s$ gives $900-6s=732$, so $s=28$ and $a=32$. Check: $480+252=732$.}

\item Three consecutive integers add to 132. What is the greatest of the three? \spr
  \ans{\\ $45$. \ Let $n$ be the smallest: $3n+3=132$, so $n=43$ and the three are
  43, 44, 45. The question asks for the greatest, not for $n$.}

\item In a club, 45\% of the members voted in an election, and 27 members voted. How
  many members does the club have? \spr
  \ans{\\ $60$. \ Let $n$ be the membership: $0.45n=27$, so $n=60$. Check:
  $0.45(60)=27$.}

\item A rectangle has a length that is 3 cm less than twice its width, and its
  perimeter is 54 cm. What is its width, in cm? \spr
  \ans{\\ $10$. \ Let $w$ be the width, so the length is $2w-3$: $2(2w-3)+2w=54$ gives
  $6w-6=54$ and $w=10$. The length is 17. Check: $2(17)+2(10)=54$.}

\item A tank holding 400 litres drains at 7 litres per minute. After how many minutes
  do 155 litres remain? \spr
  \ans{\\ $35$. \ Let $m$ be the minutes: $400-7m=155$, so $7m=245$ and $m=35$.
  Check: $400-245=155$. Reporting 245 is the trap --- that is the water lost, not the
  time.}
\end{enumerate}

% ------------------------------------------------ PART E
\PartHead{Part E --- Mixed, timed}{Calculator allowed. 12 minutes, timed.}
\sitting{Saturday}
Set a timer for 12 minutes. Answer every one --- there is no penalty for a wrong answer,
so a blank is a mark thrown away. Then check each answer by reading it back against the
story.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=20pt,itemsep=9pt]
\item A repair shop charges a flat \$25 inspection fee plus \$0.90 for each minute of
  labour. Which equation gives the total cost $C$, in dollars, of a repair taking $t$
  minutes?\\
  \makebox[4.2cm][l]{A) $C=25t+0.90$}\makebox[4.2cm][l]{B) $C=0.90t+25$}%
  \makebox[4.2cm][l]{C) $C=25.90t$}\makebox[3cm][l]{D) $C=0.90(t+25)$}
  \ans{\\ B. The per-minute charge multiplies $t$; the fee is charged once. A swaps the
  two numbers. C charges the fee every minute. D does the same inside a bracket.}

\item A shop sells pens at \$2 each and markers at \$5 each. Tom bought 12 items and
  paid \$39. How many markers did he buy? \spr
  \ans{\\ $5$. \ Let $p$ and $m$ be the counts: $p+m=12$ and $2p+5m=39$. Substituting
  gives $24+3m=39$, so $m=5$ and $p=7$. Check: $14+25=39$.}

\item A camp has 4 times as many campers $c$ as counsellors $n$. Which equation
  describes this?\\
  \makebox[3.4cm][l]{A) $4c=n$}\makebox[3.4cm][l]{B) $c=4n$}%
  \makebox[3.4cm][l]{C) $c=n+4$}\makebox[3cm][l]{D) $n=c+4$}
  \ans{\\ B. Test with 6 counsellors: 24 campers, and $24=4(6)$ holds. A is the
  direction trap. C and D read the multiplier as an addition.}

\item Twelve less than 5 times a number is 63. What is the number? \spr
  \ans{\\ $15$. \ $5x-12=63$, so $5x=75$ and $x=15$. Check: $75-12=63$. Writing
  $12-5x=63$ is the reversal trap and gives $-10.2$.}

\item A rectangle has width $w$ and a length that is 4 more than three times the width.
  Which expression gives its perimeter?\\
  \makebox[3.4cm][l]{A) $8w+8$}\makebox[3.4cm][l]{B) $4w+4$}%
  \makebox[3.4cm][l]{C) $6w+4$}\makebox[3cm][l]{D) $3w+4$}
  \ans{\\ A. The length is $3w+4$, so the perimeter is $2w+2(3w+4)=8w+8$. Check with
  $w=5$: sides 5 and 19 give 48, and $8(5)+8=48$. C doubles only the width term.
  D is the length alone. B adds the two dimensions without doubling either.}

\item A jacket is marked down by 30\% and sells for \$84. What was its original price,
  in dollars? \spr
  \ans{\\ $120$. \ A 30\% markdown leaves 70\%, so $0.70p=84$ and $p=120$. Check:
  $0.70(120)=84$. Taking 30\% of 84 and adding it gives \$109.20, which does not check.}
\end{enumerate}
