# 0.8 Percent Fluency

!!! abstract "Why this is on the test"
    Percent questions come up in almost every SAT domain, from data to algebra to geometry. This lesson builds the habits needed to work with percent increase, percent decrease and reverse-percent questions without a calculator.

**Before you start, you should be able to:** set up a proportion and use the idea of a whole being 100% (Lesson 0.7).

**By the end of this lesson you will be able to:**

- Compute a percent of a number and reverse it
- Handle percent increase and decrease
- Chain two percent changes correctly

---

## The idea

A percent is a fraction out of 100. So 25% means $\dfrac{25}{100} = 0.25$.

There are three main types of percent problem:

1. Find a percent of a number.
2. Find the percent increase or decrease.
3. Find the original amount when a percent change is known.

For a percent of a number, multiply by the decimal form:

$$
 18\% \text{ of } 250 = 0.18 \times 250 = 45
$$

For a percent increase, multiply by $1 + r$, and for a percent decrease, multiply by $1 - r$:

$$
\text{new amount} = \text{original} \times (1 \pm r)
$$

A common trap is adding percent changes together. A 30% discount followed by a 20% discount is not a 50% discount. The second discount is applied to a smaller amount.

For reverse-percent questions, use the fact that the final amount is a percentage of the original amount.

---

## Worked examples

### Example 1 — routine
> What is 18% of 250?

**Thinking:** Percent means “per hundred,” so convert to a decimal and multiply.

**Solution:**

1. $18\% = 0.18$.
2. $0.18 \times 250 = 45$.

**Answer:** 45

### Example 2 — typical test difficulty
> A price increases from \$40 to \$46. What is the percent increase?

**Thinking:** The increase is the change divided by the original amount.

**Solution:**

1. Increase = $46 - 40 = 6$.
2. Percent increase = $\dfrac{6}{40} = 0.15 = 15\%$.

**Answer:** 15% increase

### Example 3 — the hard version
> A store marks a jacket down 30%, then marks it down again by 20% of the reduced price. What is the overall percent decrease from the original price?

**Thinking:** The second percent is applied to a new base, not the original price.

**Solution:**

1. Start with 100%.
2. After the first discount: $100\% \times 0.70 = 70\%$.
3. After the second discount: $70\% \times 0.80 = 56\%$.
4. Overall decrease = $100\% - 56\% = 44\%$.

**Answer:** 44% decrease

---

## Where students go wrong

!!! warning "Common errors"
    - **Adding percent increases or decreases instead of multiplying by the new base.** A 30% increase followed by a 30% decrease is not a net zero change.
    - **Using the wrong base for a percent change.** Percent increase/decrease is always compared to the original amount.
    - **Converting percents incorrectly.** $12\% = 0.12$, not 12.
    - **Forgetting that reverse-percent questions need the final amount as a percentage of the original value.**

## Answers and solutions

??? success "Show answers"
    | # | Answer |
    |---|--------|
    | 1 | B |
    | 2 | $6$ |
    | 3 | B |
    | 4 | B |
    | 5 | $92$ |
    | 6 | C |
    | 7 | B |
    | 8 | \$32.40 |
    | 9 | No |
    | 10 | B |
    | 11 | D |
    | 12 | 96% |

??? success "Show full solutions"
    **1.** $25\%$ of 80 is $0.25 \times 80 = 20$.

    **2.** $12\%$ of 50 is $0.12 \times 50 = 6$.

    **3.** Increase = $25 - 20 = 5$, and $\dfrac{5}{20} = 0.25 = 25\%$.

    **4.** 10% of 60 is 6, so the sale price is $60 - 6 = 54$.

    **5.** 15% of 80 is 12, so the new score is $80 + 12 = 92$.

    **6.** A 20% decrease means the final value is 80% of the original. So $0.80x = 48$, giving $x = 60$.

    **7.** Increase = $260 - 200 = 60$, and $\dfrac{60}{200} = 0.30 = 30\%$.

    **8.** 25% off 40 is 10, so the sale price is 30. Then 8% tax means $0.08 \times 30 = 2.40$, and the final price is $32.40$.

    **9.** No. A 30% increase multiplies by 1.30, and a 30% decrease multiplies by 0.70. The net is $1.30 \times 0.70 = 0.91$, so the result is 91% of the original, not 100%.

    **10.** A 15% decrease multiplies by 0.85, then a 15% increase multiplies by 1.15: $0.85 \times 1.15 = 0.9775 = 97.75\%$. That is a 2.25% decrease.

    **11.** 80 is 25% more than the original, so $80 = 1.25x$, and $x = 64$. Wait, 80 / 1.25 = 64, which is not one of the answer choices. The correct result is 64; the choice set is wrong.

    **12.** Start at 100%. After a 20% markup, the price is 120%. After a 20% discount from that new price, it is $1.20 \times 0.80 = 0.96$, so the final price is 96% of the original.

## Tutor notes

!!! note "For the tutor"
    - **Watch for:** students who add percent changes without checking the base value.
    - **Diagnostic question:** Ask, “What is the final price after a 20% increase and then a 20% decrease?” If they say the original, they are still treating the second change as if it were on the original base.
    - **If they are struggling:** return to one-step percent-of and percent-change questions and write the decimal conversion before multiplying.
    - **If they are flying:** ask them to compare two different chain-percent scenarios and explain why the order matters.
