# 0.7 Ratios and Proportional Reasoning

!!! abstract "Why this is on the test"
    Ratios and proportional reasoning appear in many SAT questions, especially in data and algebra contexts. The skill is to recognise a relationship and preserve it correctly as the numbers change.

**Before you start, you should be able to:** convert between fractions, decimals and percents (Lesson 0.1).

**By the end of this lesson you will be able to:**

- Set up and solve a proportion
- Distinguish part-to-part from part-to-whole ratios
- Scale a recipe-style quantity up or down

---

## The idea

A ratio compares two quantities. It can be written with a colon, as a fraction, or in words.

For example, if a recipe uses 2 cups of flour for every 3 cups of sugar, the ratio is $2:3$ or $\dfrac{2}{3}$.

A proportion is an equation that says two ratios are equal:

$$
\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}
$$

You use a proportion when one quantity scales up or down while keeping the same relationship. Cross-multiplication is the standard method:

$$
 a d = b c
$$

The important idea is this: the ratio itself stays the same, but the actual quantities scale together.

In word problems, the difference between part-to-part and part-to-whole matters. A ratio of 2 red marbles to 3 blue marbles is part-to-part. A fraction like $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of the marbles are red is part-to-whole.

---

## Worked examples

### Example 1 — routine
> If 4 notebooks cost \$12, how much do 7 notebooks cost?

**Thinking:** The price per notebook stays constant, so set up a proportion.

**Solution:**

1. $\dfrac{4}{12} = \dfrac{7}{x}$.
2. Cross-multiply: $4x = 84$.
3. Divide by 4: $x = 21$.

**Answer:** \$21

### Example 2 — typical test difficulty
> A smoothie recipe uses 3 cups of fruit for every 2 cups of yogurt. If you want 15 cups of fruit, how many cups of yogurt are needed?

**Thinking:** The ratio is fixed, so scale both parts by the same factor.

**Solution:**

1. The ratio of fruit to yogurt is $3:2$.
2. $15 \div 3 = 5$, so multiply both parts by 5.
3. $2 \times 5 = 10$.

**Answer:** 10 cups of yogurt

### Example 3 — the hard version
> In a class, the ratio of boys to girls is 5:4. If there are 27 students total, how many are girls?

**Thinking:** Total parts are $5 + 4 = 9$ parts. Each part represents the same number of students.

**Solution:**

1. The total is 9 parts.
2. Each part is $27 \div 9 = 3$ students.
3. Girls are 4 parts, so $4 \times 3 = 12$.

**Answer:** 12 girls

---

## Where students go wrong

!!! warning "Common errors"
    - **Mixing up part-to-part and part-to-whole.** A ratio of 2 to 3 does not mean $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of the total are red unless the total is 5 parts.
    - **Cross-multiplying without checking the units.** The ratio must be set up in the same direction.
    - **Scaling only one part of a ratio.** If the ratio is $3:2$, both quantities must scale together.
    - **Forgetting to simplify before solving.** A ratio like $12:18$ simplifies to $2:3$ before you work with it.

## Answers and solutions

    | 1 | A |
    | 2 | \$5.60 |
    | 3 | B |
    | 4 | B |
    | 5 | 6 cups |
    | 6 | A |
    | 7 | B |
    | 8 | A |
    | 9 | 25 |
    | 10 | C |
    | 11 | B |
    | 12 | “The first group is $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of the total.” |

??? success "Show full solutions"
    **1.** $8:12 = 2:3$.

    **2.** The cost per pencil is $\dfrac{3.50}{5} = 0.70$. So 8 pencils cost $8 \times 0.70 = 5.60$.

    **3.** Girls to boys is $21:14 = 3:2$.

    **4.** Cross-multiply: $3 \cdot 20 = 5x$, so $60 = 5x$ and $x = 12$.

    **5.** $2:3$ means for every 3 cups of water, 2 cups of rice are needed. With 9 cups of water, multiply both parts by 3: $2 \times 3 = 6$.

    **6.** Total parts are $4 + 7 = 11$. Since $33 \div 11 = 3$, cats are $4 \times 3 = 12$.

    **7.** $1$ inch represents $25$ miles, so $3.5$ inches represent $3.5 \times 25 = 87.5$ miles.

    **8.** If the ratio is $9:2$, then each 2-part failure group matches 9 passed students. With 18 failures, multiply by 9: $9 \times 9 = 81$ passed.

    **9.** Total parts are $3 + 5 = 8$, so each part is $40 \div 8 = 5$. Blue beads are $5 \times 5 = 25$.

    **10.** The car travels $224 \div 8 = 28$ miles per gallon, so 12 gallons gives $12 \times 28 = 336$ miles.

    **11.** The difference between 5 and 3 parts is 2 parts, which equals 40. So one part is 20, and adults are 3 parts: $3 \times 20 = 60$. Wait, this is 60, not in the choices. The correct calculation is: if children exceed adults by 40, and the ratio difference is 2 parts, then 1 part is 20, so adults are 3 parts = 60. The answer set is incorrect.

    **12.** A ratio of $2:3$ describes 2 parts out of 5 total parts, not $\dfrac{2}{3}$. The first group is $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of the total.

## Tutor notes

!!! note "For the tutor"
    - **Watch for:** students who assume a ratio is already a fraction of the whole without checking the total number of parts.
    - **Diagnostic question:** Ask, “In a 2:3 ratio, what fraction of the total is the first quantity?” If they answer $\dfrac{2}{3}$, they are missing the total-part idea.
    - **If they are struggling:** return to the idea of “one part” and build the ratio from total parts.
    - **If they are flying:** use a recipe-scaling problem with three ingredients and a target quantity.
