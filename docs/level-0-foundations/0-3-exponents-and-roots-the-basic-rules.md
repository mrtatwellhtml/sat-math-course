# 0.3 Exponents and Roots: The Basic Rules

!!! abstract "Why this is on the test"
    Exponents and roots show up in advanced math, graphing and algebra questions across the digital SAT. This lesson is the foundation for polynomial work, radicals and exponential functions later in the course.

**Before you start, you should be able to:** work with negative numbers and follow the order of operations (Lesson 0.2).

**By the end of this lesson you will be able to:**

- Apply product, quotient and power rules for exponents
- Interpret negative and zero exponents
- Simplify square and cube roots of numeric expressions

---

## The idea

An exponent tells you how many times to multiply a base by itself. For example, $4^3 = 4 \times 4 \times 4 = 64$.

The most useful exponent rules are these:

$$
a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}
$$

$$
\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}
$$

$$
(a^m)^n = a^{mn}
$$

$$
a^0 = 1 \quad \text{for } a \ne 0
$$

$$
a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}
$$

The exponent laws only work when the bases match. If the bases are different, you cannot combine the terms by adding or subtracting exponents.

A square root asks, “What number times itself gives this?” So $\sqrt{49} = 7$ because $7^2 = 49$. A cube root asks, “What number times itself three times gives this?” So $\sqrt[3]{64} = 4$ because $4^3 = 64$.

To simplify a root, pull out any perfect square or perfect cube inside the radical:

$$
\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \cdot 2} = \sqrt{36}\sqrt{2} = 6\sqrt{2}
$$

This is the same idea you will use later with variables, quadratics and radicals.

## Worked examples

### Example 1 — routine
> Simplify $x^3 \cdot x^5$.

**Thinking:** Same base, so add the exponents.

**Solution:**

1. $x^3 \cdot x^5 = x^{3+5}$.
2. $x^8$.

**Answer:** $x^8$

### Example 2 — typical test difficulty
> Simplify $\dfrac{2^7}{2^3}$.

**Thinking:** Same base, so subtract exponents in division.

**Solution:**

1. $\dfrac{2^7}{2^3} = 2^{7-3}$.
2. $2^4 = 16$.

**Answer:** $16$

### Example 3 — the hard version
> Simplify $\sqrt{180}$.

**Thinking:** Look for a perfect square factor of 180.

**Solution:**

1. $180 = 36 \cdot 5$.
2. $\sqrt{180} = \sqrt{36 \cdot 5} = \sqrt{36}\sqrt{5}$.
3. $\sqrt{36} = 6$.
4. The simplified form is $6\sqrt{5}$.

**Answer:** $6\sqrt{5}$

---

## Where students go wrong

!!! warning "Common errors"
    - **Adding exponents when the bases are different.** You cannot do $2^3 \cdot 3^2 = 6^5$.
    - **Forgetting that a negative exponent is a reciprocal.** $5^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{5^2} = \dfrac{1}{25}$.
    - **Thinking $\sqrt{a+b} = \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$.** That is false.
    - **Stopping before simplifying a root completely.** $\sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$, not just $\sqrt{50}$.

??? success "Show full solutions"
    **1.** $2^3 \cdot 2^4 = 2^{3+4} = 2^7$.

    **2.** $\dfrac{5^7}{5^3} = 5^{7-3} = 5^4$.

    **3.** Any nonzero number to the zero power is 1.

    **4.** $9^2 = 81$, so $\sqrt{81} = 9$.

    **5.** $(3^2)^3 = 3^{2 \times 3} = 3^6$.

    **6.** $4^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{4^2} = \dfrac{1}{16}$.

    **7.** $48 = 16 \cdot 3$, so $\sqrt{48} = \sqrt{16}\sqrt{3} = 4\sqrt{3}$.

    **8.** $\dfrac{a^5}{a^2} = a^{5-2} = a^3$.

    **9.** $5^3 = 125$, so $\sqrt[3]{125} = 5$.

    **10.** $\dfrac{2^5 \cdot 2^3}{2^4} = \dfrac{2^8}{2^4} = 2^{8-4} = 2^4 = 16$.

    **11.** $72 = 36 \cdot 2$, so $\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36}\sqrt{2} = 6\sqrt{2}$.

    **12.** A negative exponent means reciprocal: $\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^{-2} = \left(\dfrac{3}{1}\right)^2 = 9$.

## Tutor notes

!!! note "For the tutor"
    - **Watch for:** students who apply exponent rules to unlike bases or who forget that a negative exponent means reciprocal.
    - **Diagnostic question:** Ask, “What is $2^{-3}$?” If they say $-8$, they are missing the reciprocal rule.
    - **If they are struggling:** return to repeated multiplication with small numbers before formal rules.
    - **If they are flying:** ask them to simplify $\dfrac{(x^2)^3}{x^4}$ and explain each step.
