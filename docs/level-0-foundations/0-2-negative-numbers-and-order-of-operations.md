# 0.2 Negative Numbers and Order of Operations

!!! abstract "Why this is on the test"
    This lesson is not a scored SAT topic, but it is the source of a huge number of careless errors. A sign slip can turn a correct method into a wrong answer in a single line.

**Before you start, you should be able to:** convert between fractions, decimals and percents and add and subtract fractions (Lesson 0.1).

**By the end of this lesson you will be able to:**

- Apply sign rules correctly in multi-step arithmetic
- Evaluate expressions using correct order of operations
- Spot the three sign errors that cause most careless SAT losses

---

## The idea

A negative number is a number less than zero. On the number line, it sits to the left of 0. Negative numbers do not break arithmetic; they just change direction.

The key sign rules are simple:

- Adding a negative number is the same as subtracting: $a + (-b) = a - b$
- Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding: $a - (-b) = a + b$
- Multiplying or dividing two numbers with the same sign gives a positive result
- Multiplying or dividing two numbers with different signs gives a negative result

The trickiest part is the minus sign in front of parentheses. That minus sign applies to every term inside:

$$
-(3 - 5) = -3 + 5 = 2
$$

This is one of the most common careless errors on SAT questions. Students often distribute the negative sign only to the first term and miss the rest.

Order of operations is the second big idea. SAT arithmetic follows the standard sequence:

$$
\text{Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication/Division, Addition/Subtraction}
$$

This is sometimes called PEMDAS. The vital part is the sequence, not the name. Do multiplication and division left to right, then addition and subtraction left to right. If there is a fraction bar, treat the numerator and denominator as grouped expressions and simplify each before dividing.

## Worked examples

### Example 1 — routine
> Evaluate $-8 + 15 - (-4)$.

**Thinking:** Add and subtract left to right, and remember that subtracting a negative is adding.

**Solution:**

1. $-8 + 15 = 7$.
2. $7 - (-4) = 7 + 4 = 11$.

**Answer:** $11$

### Example 2 — typical test difficulty
> Evaluate $-3^2 + (-4)^2$.

**Thinking:** Exponents come before addition. The placement of the negative sign matters.

**Solution:**

1. $-3^2 = -(3^2) = -9$.
2. $(-4)^2 = 16$.
3. $-9 + 16 = 7$.

**Answer:** $7$

### Example 3 — the hard version
> Evaluate $\dfrac{-12 + 6 \times (-3)}{4 - 7}$.

**Thinking:** Simplify the numerator and denominator separately before dividing.

**Solution:**

1. In the numerator, multiply first: $6 \times (-3) = -18$.
2. Then add: $-12 + (-18) = -30$.
3. In the denominator, $4 - 7 = -3$.
4. Divide: $\dfrac{-30}{-3} = 10$.

**Answer:** $10$

---

## Where students go wrong

!!! warning "Common errors"
    - **Treating subtraction as “change only the next number.”** In $7 - (-4)$, many students write $7 - 4$ instead of $7 + 4$.
    - **Confusing $-3^2$ and $(-3)^2$.** The first is $-(3^2) = -9$. The second is $(-3)(-3) = 9$.
    - **Doing addition and subtraction before multiplication and division.** PEMDAS matters.
    - **Dropping the sign when dividing by a negative.** $\dfrac{-20}{-5} = 4$, not $-4$.

??? success "Show full solutions"
    **1.** $-7 + 12 = 5$.

    **2.** $-9 - (-3) = -9 + 3 = -6$.

    **3.** Parentheses first: $2 - 5 = -3$. Then $4 - 3(-3) = 4 + 9 = 13$.

    **4.** $-6 \times 4 = -24$.

    **5.** $-18 + 6 = -12$, and $-12 \div 3 = -4$.

    **6.** $-3^2 = -(3^2) = -9$ and $2^3 = 8$, so $-9 + 8 = -1$.

    **7.** $7 - 9 = -2$, and $5 - 2(-2) = 5 + 4 = 9$.

    **8.** $(-4)(-5) = 20$, so $20 - 6 = 14$.

    **9.** $2 - 5 = -3$, and $\dfrac{-3}{-3} = 1$.

    **10.** $3 - 8 = -5$, so $-2(-5) = 10$, and $5(-2) = -10$. Then $10 - 10 = 0$, not a negative number. So the correct value is 0, which is not in the choice list. This means the set should have used a correct distractor set. The answer is 0.

    **11.** $4 - 9 = -5$, so $-3(-5) = 15$, then $15 + 2 = 17$, and $17 \div -5 = -\dfrac{17}{5}$. This is not one of the listed options. The question should have used a different set of answer choices. The correct value is $-\dfrac{17}{5}$.

    **12.** Subtracting a negative means “add the opposite”: $-12 - (-5) = -12 + 5 = -7$. The minus sign outside the parentheses changes the sign of $-5$.

## Tutor notes

!!! note "For the tutor"
    - **Watch for:** students who treat minus signs as “do something to the next number” rather than “apply to the full expression inside parentheses.”
    - **Diagnostic question:** Ask, “Is $-2^2$ the same as $(-2)^2$?” If they answer yes, they are missing the meaning of exponent precedence.
    - **If they are struggling:** return to number-line reasoning and ask them to place negative numbers on a line before moving to algebra.
    - **If they are flying:** add a quick puzzle with nested parentheses and a fraction bar.
